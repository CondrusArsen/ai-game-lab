How it works

1Scan expiring contracts
2Match weight and class to a truck
3Price empty and loaded legs
4Advance time and monitor the route
5Reinvest in fuel, service, trucks, and depots

Contracts are time-bounded financial objects. Each exposes an acceptance window, delivery time, payment, weight, truck class, distance, and a map preview. A late load pays only 60% of the promise. Crossing the Bosphorus adds a 350 TL bridge toll that is reimbursed in the contract payment.

Position creates hidden cost. If a truck is not at pickup, the game builds a multi-leg trip: empty to cargo, then loaded to destination. The interface calculates distance, market fuel, maintenance, total cost, net profit, and estimated arrival before acceptance. The tutorial explicitly says the goal is a profitable route—not a profitable-looking contract.

The fleet is differentiated. Light trucks carry 2–8 tons, medium 8–20, heavy 20–45. Each model trades purchase price, capacity, speed, fuel consumption, and maintenance per kilometre. Wear increases breakdown risk to 15% at 100%; depots automatically remove 15% wear when a truck visits.

Operating systems exposed

  • Contracts with filters and route preview
  • 1× / 8× / 16× time controls
  • Daily fuel market and stock tank
  • Vehicle-level revenue and period reports
  • Depots with daily cost and local bonus
  • Idle repair and emergency service
  • Bank loans at 3% monthly interest
  • Autosave, manual save, language, and currency

What the recorded session proved

00:00–01:17

Settings, company name, Ankara headquarters, and an 11-step tutorial cover contracts, late penalties, route preview, multi-leg transfers, bridge tolls, truck classes, fuel, finance, bank debt, wear, depot service, and the profitable-route principle.

01:17–02:49

The player opens the full-country map, inspects depots and seven truck models, buys a first Ford Transit 350L, opens contracts, and discovers that attractive gross payments can still carry delivery risk or an expensive empty leg.

02:49–04:47

At 16× speed, routes animate across Turkey. A second light truck is added, cargo chains are accepted, and vehicle detail begins to show total distance, earnings, expenses, margin, wear, location, and relative fleet ranking.

04:47–07:56

The company expands to four depots and two trucks. Fuel is bought while the market price rises; the finance ledger records deliveries and operating costs. The run ends with $1,827 cash, both trucks on empty legs, and a successful manual save.

Strong · pre-commitment economics

The contract modal turns strategy into an inspectable equation before the player clicks Accept. Empty travel, fuel, maintenance, deadline, and net profit are not hidden post-hoc punishments.

Strong · geography is systemic

Headquarters output, depot bonuses, bridge tolls, city position, and return cargo make the real map more than a background.

Friction · too many panels before delight

The opening explains nearly every subsystem before the player has completed one shipment. The conceptual load is accurate, but the first satisfying receipt arrives late.

Friction · dual currency needs explanation

The company balance can be displayed in dollars while diesel remains priced in TL/L and converted purchases appear in the ledger. Without an explicit exchange-rate cue, valid conversion can read like inconsistency.

Recommendations

Guide one closed loop

Preselect one Ankara contract with a guaranteed return load, then reveal gross, empty-leg cost, net, delivery, and the next cargo as a single first-day story.

Progressively disclose the tutorial

Teach late penalties when one first becomes possible, fuel when the tank matters, and wear after a completed route. Keep the full reference reopenable.

Make network health visual

Summarize loaded kilometres, empty ratio, margin, deadlines at risk, and depot coverage in one top-level strip so 16× speed remains readable.

Overlays & interruptions

  • Company creation with name and HQ-city selection.
  • Tutorial coachmarks that lead through map, contract calculation, assignment, and time controls.
  • Contract economics sheet with empty/loaded legs, gross payout, costs, deadline, penalty, and net.
  • Fleet, truck, city/depot, and finance panels synchronized with the same selected map object.

Motion, VFX & feedback

  • Selected routes brighten while inactive roads recede; empty and cargo legs use distinct line patterns.
  • Truck markers interpolate along roads and expose arrival/loading/service stops rather than teleporting.
  • Income, expense, late-risk, low-fuel, and high-wear events use signed toasts plus persistent panel state.
  • Map selection, depot construction, and delivery completion receive concise pulses, avoiding arcade-scale distraction.
  • Simulation speed changes alter motion/timer rate consistently and display the multiplier at all times.

Responsive, accessibility & edge cases

  • Map objects require keyboard/list equivalents; dense geographic hit targets cannot be mouse-only.
  • Use patterns and labels in addition to route color, especially for empty, loaded, late, and selected paths.
  • On narrow screens, make the map full-stage and convert management rails into bottom sheets with a visible selected-object breadcrumb.
  • Pause on confirmation dialogs and guarantee single resolution for accept, arrival, service, purchase, and deadline events.
Reconstruction test. A player should create a company, find and evaluate a contract, explain gross versus net, assign an eligible truck, watch empty and loaded travel, detect a late/fuel/wear risk, complete delivery, service the vehicle, and reconcile every cash movement in the ledger.

Reconstruction blueprint

What must exist to rebuild the observed game

This is a product and systems specification derived from the public build and the recorded first session. It describes observable behaviour, not the original source-code architecture.

Reconstruction confidence. High for onboarding, contracts, routing, truck classes, fuel/wear, finance, depots, and the first four simulated days. Medium for mature fleets, bankruptcy, and long-term market behaviour.

State machineSession flow

  • Company setup → name the firm and choose a headquarters city with regional production context.
  • Market scan → inspect available contracts, deadlines, class requirements, payment, and route preview.
  • Assignment → choose an eligible truck; calculate its empty positioning leg and loaded delivery leg.
  • Simulation → advance at 1×/8×/16× while trucks move, consume fuel, accrue maintenance, and cross toll links.
  • Delivery → credit payment or apply the visible 60% late penalty; update ledger, wear, and truck location.
  • Reinvestment → purchase trucks, fuel, depots, service, or loans, then seek a profitable return chain.

Input contractControls

  • Mouse selects cities, contracts, trucks, depots, fuel, finance rows, and map routes.
  • Space pauses; 1×, 8×, and 16× control simulation speed.
  • Map zoom and pan expose the continental road graph and moving fleet.
  • Manual save is present; the recording did not perform a reload-persistence test.

Rules engineSimulation and resolution

  • A contract specifies origin, destination, cargo, weight, delivery time, payment, and a truck-class requirement.
  • The route preview separates empty positioning from loaded travel and itemises fuel, maintenance, tolls, and net profit.
  • Late delivery cuts payment by 60%.
  • Light trucks handle 2–8 t, Medium 8–20 t, and Heavy 20–45 t in the tutorial text.
  • The Bosporus/bridge link exposes a 350 TL toll in the captured tutorial.
  • Wear can cause breakdown after 15% and reaches full deterioration at 100%; service, idle repair, and depots reduce it.
  • A profitable route is not automatically a profitable contract: empty repositioning and the return leg determine the real margin.

Meta layerEconomy and progression

  • Start with one city and a small vehicle; build coverage through more trucks and depots.
  • Larger classes unlock heavier cargo but add purchase and operating exposure.
  • Fuel buying, maintenance, tolls, depot costs, and loan interest compete with headline contract revenue.
  • Loans carry 3% monthly interest in the tutorial; the ledger makes financing and route mistakes auditable.
  • Four depots and two Ford Transit trucks were reached in the recorded session, not a mature network.

Minimum useful data model

  • CityNode: coordinates, region, produced cargo, depot ownership, fuel price, contract-generation weights.
  • RoadEdge: distance, speed/time, toll, bridge/ferry flags, routing cost.
  • TruckState: class, capacity, location, route legs, cargo, fuel consumption, wear, breakdown/service state.

Production sequence

Build the causal loop before the content surface

A practical implementation order for reproducing the observed promise while keeping the prototype testable at every step.

  1. Implement a city graph, shortest-route solver, simulated clock, one truck, and origin-to-destination movement.
  2. Add contract generation, class/weight validation, deadlines, late penalties, and an itemised route quote.
  3. Add empty repositioning, fuel, tolls, maintenance, wear/breakdowns, and a transaction ledger.
  4. Add fleet purchases, depots, repair, loans, regional cargo supply, time controls, and save/load.
  5. Balance via automated route-chain tests that include the next empty leg, not only single-contract margin.

Boundaries and unresolved behaviour

Do not encode these as facts yet.
  • Market refresh rules, cargo scarcity, AI competitors, and mature-network scaling were not established.
  • The precise breakdown probability above 15% wear and every repair/depot modifier need targeted tests.
  • Currency presentation mixed a dollar balance with TL-per-litre fuel labels; the intended economic denomination is unclear.
