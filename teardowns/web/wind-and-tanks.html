01
Executive verdict

A strong shot simulator trapped inside an unreliable turn handoff.

Observed

The formula

Choose a battlefield and rule mix, reposition a tank, set an exact barrel angle, charge power, compensate for directional wind, then let projectile impact and destructible terrain rewrite the next turn.

Visible

The differentiator

The control panel exposes the variables that artillery games usually hide: numeric angle, previous angle, previous power, move and energy budgets, shell costs, wind direction and strength. The game feels inspectable, not arbitrary.

Observed

The release blocker

The CPU phase stopped progressing in two attempts. With no timeout, retry or diagnostic state, the match cannot reliably reach damage, victory or rematch—the moments that validate every system above it.

Study it? Yes—for variable-rich browser combat, configurable content and HUD architecture. Reconstruct it as a vertical slice only after making turn resolution deterministic and recoverable.

03
What the game is

Worms-like ballistics recast as a configurable browser control room.

Visible Wind & Tanks is a side-view, turn-based tank artillery game. A full arena sits above a dense instrument panel; the player trades movement and energy for positional, ballistic and item decisions.

Session shape

Short-to-medium tactical matches, with each turn built from observation, fine adjustment, committed charge and a projectile-resolution beat.

Fantasy

Operate a compact tank like a field engineer: read the atmosphere, calculate a shot and exploit terrain rather than rely on twitch aim.

Audience

Players who enjoy artillery classics, systems-heavy browser games, local experimentation and online party competition.

Scope on offer

CPU levels 1–5; 1v1, 2v2, 3v3 and free-for-all; multiple stage themes, ammo types, airdrops, mines, range tools and lighting rules.

04
Observed first session

Configuration was rich; the first complete match never arrived.

Land

Read the beta notice, activity totals and account incentive; choose offline CPU Battle because no online activity is visible.

Configure

Inspect CPU level, match size and a deep advanced-settings catalog before starting a 1v1 match.

Learn by HUD

Enter a night arena, move, rotate the barrel and discover that the lower panel tracks energy, movement, wind and shell costs.

Commit

Charge an AP shot and release it. The impact produces a large circular blast and permanently removes terrain.

Probe systems

Pass a turn, observe airdropped objects and a mine-chain message, then open controls, help and the integrated bug report.

Hit the boundary

Exit and restart. In both attempts the CPU phase stops advancing, so damage, victory, results and rematch remain unobserved.

Decision input

Target position, intervening geometry, wind direction and magnitude, remaining movement, energy price and the prior shot’s angle/power.

Committed action

Charging exposes a 0–100 power meter. Releasing creates a projectile; the exact shot cannot be adjusted in flight in the observed slice.

Repeat reason

Every impact changes the aiming problem. Variable wind, terrain loss, drops, mines and shell options prevent the next calculation from being identical.

Unknown The loop’s intended terminal condition, damage formula, elimination order and post-match cadence were not reached. They must not be inferred from genre conventions.

08
Progression, economy and content

Variety is broad; persistent progression is not evidenced.

Visible

Within-turn resources

HP, ENERGY, MOVE, selected shell/item, exact angle and stored last-shot values define the tactical state. Weapon “Cost” is an energy price, not a monetized currency.

Visible

Match variation

CPU level, player format, stage themes, airdrop events, ammo types, mines, range chart, blackout lights and starting positions can be configured.

Unknown

Meta progression

No unlock tree, XP, inventory ownership, rank reward, currency store or persistent loadout was observed. Do not add them for parity.

Expansion cost. New shells, maps and events are not independent content drops: each multiplies ballistics, terrain, AI, lighting, team-mode and online synchronization QA. The content surface is attractive but operationally expensive.
09
UX/UI reconstruction atlas

Four screen families, one dense battle instrument.

Dimensions are relational. The observed composition is desktop-first and framed around a fixed-width 16:9 battlefield above a persistent control panel.

A · Landing / beta hub

Purpose: establish beta status, route to online or CPU play, surface activity and support links.

Composition: illustrated hill backdrop; centered logo; large announcement and gift cards; two mode columns; footer links; floating language/fullscreen/audio pills.

Visible copy: “Online Battle,” “CPU Battle,” “Local Total,” “Online Total,” beta reset/account carry-over notice.

States: activity empty, online availability, signed-out/signed-in, announcement present/expired.

B · CPU setup / advanced rules

Purpose: select difficulty and match format; optionally tune content.

Composition: centered setup stack over landscape; segmented level and player controls; collapsible advanced catalog; dominant orange Start Battle.

Visible copy: “CPU Battle,” “Level,” “Players,” “Advanced Settings,” “Start Battle,” “Back.”

States: levels 1–5, 1v1/2v2/3v3/FFA, collapsed/expanded, preset and per-chip selection.

C · Battle

Purpose: show world outcome and expose every committed variable.

Composition: HP bars at top corners; actor toast top-center; battlefield ≈72% of game height; lower panel with turn/wind row, resource gauges, angle dial, shells and item toolbar.

States: player turn, charging, projectile flight, impact resolution, pass toast, CPU turn, item drop, mine chain, stalled CPU.

D · Utility overlays

Purpose: teach controls, explain play, collect beta bug detail.

Composition: Controls as scrollable left modal, Help as narrow right rail, Report as centered modal with dimmed battlefield.

States: open/closed, scrolled, field focus, category selected, attachment included/removed, submitting, error/success unseen.

10
Feedback and presentation

Every shot has spectacle; every system needs a clearer resolved state.

Readable commitment

Charge fill, exact angle, wind capsule, selected shell and resource bars are visible before release. “Last” values let the next shot build on memory.

Readable world change

Large dark blast circles, debris and circular terrain cuts make impact legible even when the projectile is small. Airdrop silhouettes and boxed pickup icons signal new opportunities.

Readable events

Turn banners and red toasts name pass and mine-chain events. The CPU stall is the exception: the screen continues to look like a legitimate thinking state with no elapsed-time cue.

Identity

Night-sky calm, toy-like tanks and heavy instrument panels create a “serious calculations, playful hardware” tone. Orange/cyan highlights keep the dark interface lively.

Motion

Unknown Frames establish charge, projectile, explosion and falling-event states, not animation duration, easing or camera shake. Rebuild them as fast, readable phases rather than inventing cinematic timing.

Sound

The UI exposes a volume level marked “High,” so audio settings exist. Specific shot, hit, warning and ambience assets were not established by the allowed evidence.

Simulation contract

  • Use fixed-step physics and a seeded event RNG.
  • Store signed wind once per turn and feed the same vector to HUD and projectile.
  • Resolve blast → terrain → chain effects → tank settlement → damage in a documented order.
  • Record last committed angle and power only after shot acceptance.

Reliability contract

  • Give CPU planning and simulation separate deadlines.
  • On AI timeout, select a legal fallback shot or pass.
  • Expose “CPU is thinking,” elapsed threshold and recovery action.
  • Persist a compact replayable turn log for bug reproduction.

Accessibility contract

  • All actions reachable by keyboard with visible focus.
  • Never encode selected/disabled state by color alone.
  • Announce turn, wind, toast and HP changes via live regions.
  • Pause nonessential motion under reduced-motion preference.

Unknown boundary

  • Do not invent damage, energy regeneration or scoring formulas.
  • Do not claim original networking or physics architecture.
  • Do not build ranked progression for parity.
  • Implement one explicit result contract until original is observed.

Vertical slices

1 · Ballistic labOne tank, wind, angle, charge, projectile and impact marker with deterministic replay.

Acceptance specification

Prove the turn finishes before polishing the explosion.

Landing. Given a fresh visitor, when the page loads, then the Wind & Tanks logo, beta notice, activity summaries, Online Battle, CPU Battle and global language/fullscreen/audio controls are visible.
CPU setup. Given CPU Battle, when it opens, then levels 1–5, 1v1/2v2/3v3/FFA, collapsed Advanced Settings, Start Battle and Back are keyboard reachable.
Advanced configuration. Given expanded settings, when presets or chips change, then selected states are distinguishable beyond color and the resulting concrete config is inspectable.
Battle initialization. Given a valid config, when Start Battle is activated, then two tanks, HP 100 bars, active actor, wind, POWER/ENERGY/MOVE, angle, ammo and utilities render without layout shift.
Angle parity. Given player control, when mouse or keys change aim, then barrel pose, radial pointer and decimal value remain identical representations of one value.
Movement budget. Given MOVE above zero, when the tank translates, then position changes and MOVE decreases; hull rotation remains legal at zero if the visible rule is enabled.
Charge and fire. Given AP selected, when Space or pointer is held and released, then power fills to a 0–100 cap, input locks once, a projectile spawns and Last power records the committed value.
Wind authority. Given a signed wind value, when a shot is simulated, then HUD arrows/strength and projectile acceleration use exactly that same value.
Energy gating. Given insufficient energy for a shell, when selection is attempted, then it remains unavailable with an accessible reason and no energy is spent.
Terrain resolution. Given an impact on terrain, when the blast resolves, then visual and collision geometry remove the same region and unsupported tanks settle before the next actor begins.
Pass. Given a player turn, when Pass is confirmed, then a visible/announced pass message appears and exactly one turn transition begins.
Chain event. Given linked mines, when one detonates, then all queued blasts resolve deterministically, the mine-chain message appears and control does not transfer mid-chain.
CPU completion. Given a CPU turn, when planning succeeds, then a legal action resolves; when its deadline expires, then a fallback action or explicit Retry/Exit recovery prevents soft lock.
Controls and Help. Given battle, when Keys/Help is opened, then its scrollable copy appears, focus is contained, Escape closes it and focus returns to the invoker without mutating the turn.
Report. Given battle context, when Report opens, then category, required details, optional email, battlefield attachment consent, preview, technical disclosure, Cancel and Send states are available.
Exit. Given a live match, when Exit is used, then loss of match state is made explicit and the route returns without leaving simulation work running.
Result boundary. Given one tank reaches the implemented terminal rule, when resolution completes, then input locks and a minimal winner/result/restart contract appears; copy remains an implementation choice until original evidence exists.
Visual regression. Compare landing, CPU setup, advanced list, player turn, charging, impact, CPU turn, Controls, Help and Report at wide and narrow desktop widths; no essential HUD element may overlap or leave the viewport.
18
Experiments

Fix certainty first; then reduce the cost of learning.

Hypothesis 01

CPU watchdog converts abandonment into completion.

Test: compare current build with a visible thinking state, bounded AI deadline and automatic fallback pass/shot plus Retry turn.

Success metric: soft-lock rate, completed CPU matches, recovery success and reports per 100 CPU turns.

False-reading risk: fallback may increase completion while making CPU feel strategically worse; track quality complaints separately.

Hypothesis 02

A Quick Battle path gets players to the first shot faster.

Test: default to Standard 1v1 with one CTA; move the full rules matrix behind Customize.

Success metric: time to first shot, setup abandonment, advanced-settings reopen rate and first-match completion.

False-reading risk: lower setup time may hide demand from expert players; segment new and returning users.

Hypothesis 03

A guided first-shot layer makes the dense HUD feel empowering.

Test: sequentially highlight wind, angle, power and release; retain an always-available “Why did that miss?” explanation using last-shot values.

Success metric: input errors, time to valid shot, second-shot accuracy delta and Help opens before/after the first shot.

False-reading risk: better accuracy may come from slower, over-instructed play; measure turn duration and skip rate.

Hypothesis 04

Trajectory memory improves mastery without removing skill.

Test: offer an optional faint previous-shot arc or impact marker after the first attempt, never a future trajectory preview.

Success metric: adjustment quality, hit rate over turns, match duration and opt-out rate.

False-reading risk: terrain change can make the previous arc misleading; invalidate it visibly when geometry changes materially.

Hypothesis 05

Compressing the beta notice preserves trust and restores play hierarchy.

Test: reduce the landing announcement to one status line with expandable release notes; keep report and reset warnings accessible.

Success metric: CPU/Online CTA visibility without scroll, mode-selection time and notice expansion rate.

False-reading risk: fewer notice reads may increase surprise about score resets; monitor related support reports.

Confidence and unknowns

High confidence in the instrument panel; no confidence in the match ending.

High confidence

  • Landing hierarchy and beta copy.
  • CPU setup options and advanced-setting labels.
  • Player-turn HUD composition.
  • Angle, power, wind, movement and shell-cost displays.
  • Pass, projectile impact, terrain destruction and mine-chain states.
  • Controls, Help and Report overlays.
  • Repeated non-advancing CPU phase.

Medium confidence

  • Orange chips represent enabled/selected options consistently.
  • Airdrops follow a repeatable turn cadence.
  • Totals are match scoring rather than another activity measure.
  • CPU levels change decision quality rather than timing only.
  • Account continuity includes score/history beyond identity.

Unknown

  • Damage, armor, fall and elimination formulas.
  • Energy recharge and item-consumption rules.
  • Win/result/rematch UI and scoring semantics.
  • Online lobby, chat, reconnection and synchronization behavior.
  • Mobile/touch support and exact region/device matrix.
  • Persistence, privacy and score lifecycle.
  • Audio asset set and motion timing.
  • Engine, libraries, backend, hosting and analytics.

Evidence boundary

Observed The product path, interactions and repeated CPU stall come from direct session behavior. Visible Exact labels, values, layout, configuration options and overlay copy come from captured interface states. Radar Developer identity, launch-post metrics, public mode claims and dated release context come from the source snapshot. Hypothesis Growth explanations and experiments are testable interpretations. Unknown Hidden formulas, terminal states and architecture are not asserted.

Bottom line. Reconstruct the observed version as a deterministic offline 1v1 artillery slice with a faithful instrument panel, destructible terrain, wind, pass, one airdrop, one chain event and robust CPU recovery. Treat online play, full content parity and the original result flow as later evidence-gated work.
