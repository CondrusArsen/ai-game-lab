Web Teardown
Product analysis · reconstruction-grade UX/UI specification

Ringhold

A compact node-control RTS where every line is both a supply chain and an invasion route. The build is strategically legible, unusually complete for a browser release, and held back chiefly by a first-minute interaction model that asks the player to learn too many meanings for the same pointer gesture.

01 · Executive verdict

A real strategy game hiding inside a one-drag interface

Ringhold compresses territory, economy, logistics, defense and army movement into a readable network. Its strongest feature is systemic density per screen; its weakest is that the game exposes the whole command language before the player has formed a mental model.

8/10System clarity after learning
5/10First-minute clarity
9/10Scope-to-complexity ratio
6/10Return motivation observed
Product thesis. The player should feel like a field commander drawing intent across a living front, not like an operator discovering hidden pointer semantics. Preserve the systemic depth; stage its command vocabulary.
02 · Why the concept works

One topology, several games

Readable state

Ownership, army size, route connectivity, strongholds and incoming forces coexist on a single map. The player rarely needs a separate management screen during combat.

Meaningful geometry

Graph position creates strategy: rear nodes feed the front, chokepoints amplify fortification, and branching paths create flank risk without a free-moving camera.

Elastic tempo

Pause plus 0.5×/1×/2×/4× lets deliberative players inspect the graph while experienced players compress low-decision periods.

Core fantasy

“Hold the line, then take theirs” is not only marketing language. It maps directly to the state machine: survive raids, accumulate surplus, convert neutral territory, establish supply, fortify the new edge, and eventually collapse enemy strongholds.

03 · Product definition

Positioning, audience and mode architecture

One-line pitch

A pause-friendly real-time strategy game about sending armies through a graph, capturing forts, building a frontier and keeping your Ringhold alive under escalating raids.

Primary audience

Desktop players who enjoy compact systems games, graph conquest, tower-defense pressure and runs that fit into a browser session.

Likely session promise

Fast entry, one-screen strategic overview, short tactical arcs, shareable seeded maps, and escalating mastery rather than content-heavy spectacle.

Platform posture

The observed interface assumes a pointer, hover-like inspection, dense fine text and keyboard accelerators. Mobile support is not evidenced.

04 · Observed session

The six-minute experience arc

DiscoverStore page frames the fantasy and launches in-browser.
OrientMain menu exposes four paths and global audio.
StudyField guide reveals a deep command and tech vocabulary.
FailFirst mission reaches its final wave but the Ringhold falls.
AdaptRetry uses distributed sends and clears the mission.
ContinuePost-win continuation extends the same map beyond the quota.
ExploreFreeplay tests longer escalation, speed and automation.

What the first failure teaches

Survival is not achieved by passive stockpiling. Every captured node can become a depth buffer, supply outlet and target that absorbs enemy strength before it reaches the Ringhold.

What the retry confirms

The game rewards route-wide distribution and timing. Even low-count garrisons delay a raid and let rear production continue flowing forward.

What freeplay adds

Larger graphs and sustained waves shift attention from a single objective to route maintenance, recovery and choosing which front deserves reinforcement.

05 · Core loop

Accumulate → route → capture → build → hold

1 · AccumulateOwned settlements generate troops; the Ringhold carries health and a garrison.
2 · RouteSend forces along network edges or establish supply toward an exposed front.
3 · CaptureOvercome neutral or hostile garrisons; ownership recolors the node.
4 · DevelopTap owned nodes to build or upgrade economy, attack or defense roles.
5 · HoldAbsorb periodic raids, repair damage and maintain depth.
6 · ConvertUse the stable rear to feed the next frontier or assault a Ringhold.

Moment-to-moment decisions

  • How many troops can leave without exposing a node?
  • Which neutral node creates the best next branching position?
  • Should a route stay as active supply or stop accumulating at destination?
  • Is a wave better answered with depth, a stronger fort, or an offensive counter-push?
  • When should the simulation be accelerated?
  • Should grouped nodes send together, or should one strong source carry the action?
  • Can an enemy spawn be silenced before its next army forms?
  • Is the stronger payoff survival, total conquest, or continuing after the quota?
06 · Controls & interaction model

A compact command language with overloaded gestures

InputObserved / documented resultRequired feedback
Drag from nodeSend troops; holding still charges the committed amountSource halo, charge amount, destination preview, route line, release affordance
Tap owned nodeOpen build or upgrade actionSelection state and contextual build menu; suppress accidental sends
Hold enemyStrike from nearest friendly fortsSource candidates and combined force preview
Hold friendlyRally friendly neighboursParticipating nodes highlighted before commit
Shift-click nodesCreate group; drag once to send together; Escape clearsPersistent group tint and count
Double-tap groupHold to rallyGroup selection confirmation
Alt + sendCommit every available troopExplicit “all-in” warning state
Space / 0 / 1 / 2 / 4 / − / +Pause and set simulation speedTop-bar active state, keyboard focus-independent
AToggle co-pilot in freeplay or skirmishAutomation badge plus scoring consequence
Critical onboarding defect. The same pointer can mean inspect, build, charge, send, rally or multi-source attack. The field guide documents this accurately, but documentation does not replace staged motor learning. The first mission should gate these meanings one at a time.

Recommended interaction hierarchy

  1. Click = select. A selected node always shows its role, garrison, production and available actions.
  2. Drag = transfer. The player sees a live fraction/number before release; cancel by returning to source or pressing Escape.
  3. Hold = charge only after drag threshold. Avoid a stationary press changing mode before intent is legible.
  4. Contextual buttons teach advanced gestures. “Rally neighbours” and “Strike” remain clickable while surfacing their shortcuts.
07 · Rules & economy

Reconstruction model

Territory

The map is an undirected graph rendered over an island. Nodes are forts/settlements, edges are legal march routes, and Ringholds are faction anchors with health.

Combat

Armies travel on edges and reduce defenders on arrival. Raiders must capture and garrison intervening nodes, so territorial depth consumes their force.

Supply

Full rear settlements automatically feed open positions toward the front. Blue chevrons identify an active supply route. Sending from the source, or filling the receiver, stops the flow.

SystemRule visible in productImplementation note
OwnershipBlue = player, red = enemy, gray = neutral; node icon and flag change with factionRepresent as factionId plus capture/garrison state; never rely on color alone.
RingholdPrimary base has health, garrison and a glow/radius; damage can be repaired at a troop cost, not above original strengthSeparate hit points from stationed troops. Display both adjacent to the base.
WavesCount and countdown shown in HUD; final “horde” receives high-salience warningWave director owns pre-warning, spawn composition and result checks.
SpawnEnemy spawn nodes launch attacks and can be permanently silenced by captureDistinct spawn capability flag; destruction/capture state must persist during run.
StructuresBuild once, level through I–III, then fuse; fused structures keep rankingNode building slot with family, level and fused subtype; razing returns a partial salvage.
Army classesScout, company and host communicate size; strike forces originate from barracks/bunkersPresentation can bucket an exact count into silhouettes while retaining the numeric label.

Technology tree

Economy

Farm I–III → Keep. Faster troop growth culminating in a fortified economic node.

Offense

Barracks I–III → Bunker. Armored/strike forces; advanced form is an offensive strongpoint.

Defense

Archer → Wall → Turret → Cannon. Ranged attrition, slowing/deflection, concentrated fire and node bombardment are presented as related defensive tools.

08 · Progression & difficulty

Authored curriculum outside, emergent escalation inside

Campaign progression

Twenty named missions unlock sequentially. Early missions teach fixed wave quotas; later names and descriptions introduce rival Ringholds, multiple attackers, longer holds and total-war conditions. Results award up to three stars.

Run progression

Each map expands through captured territory, upgraded structures and stronger supply. Enemy pressure escalates by wave count and army strength. The run ends when the Ringhold falls or the mode’s conquest/survival condition resolves.

10 · Feedback hierarchy

The map is the HUD; overlays should explain, not compete

PrioritySignalCurrent patternSpecification

Visual language

Near-black chrome isolates the tactical map. Warm gold denotes selection, instruction and primary actions; blue denotes the player; red denotes hostile pressure and failure; gray denotes neutral territory. Pixel terrain is softened by glow and vignette while UI remains crisp monospaced text.

Audio language

Separate master, music and effects sliders are available from the shell. A faithful rebuild should reserve distinct cues for wave arrival, capture, route commit, stronghold damage and terminal outcome. Exact source sounds were not assessed.

11 · Return loop & retention

Seeds, stars and “one more map”

Short-term return

Retry preserves the mission/map identity; replay, continue and next-mission actions are adjacent to the result, reducing recovery friction after both failure and success.

Mid-term return

Twenty named campaign missions form a visible backlog. Locks establish forward momentum while stars suggest optional mastery.

Long-term return

Procedural freeplay islands, map seeds, multiple AI opponents and share actions can support challenge exchange without content authoring for every session.

Retention gaps

  • No observed persistent profile, cross-session statistics, daily challenge, achievement surface or explicit metagame economy.
  • Stars are visible, but their thresholds and any cumulative reward are not surfaced in the observed path.
  • Sharing exists at result screens, yet the payload and recipient experience were not exercised.
  • Freeplay’s automated co-pilot changes scoring status; this is disclosed only strongly at the result, after the choice has already affected the run.
Best retention extension: a daily seeded island with fixed difficulty and a small, non-monetary leaderboard. It reinforces the game’s native strengths—comparable topology, compact sessions and shareable outcomes—without introducing an unrelated progression economy.

  • Creator-authored WebGames launch post.

  • Clear visual/pitch hook and no download barrier.

What is not verified

  • Independent reposting or multi-community spread.
  • Creator audience leverage.
  • Meaningful player volume or retention.
  • Press, platform featuring or durable social momentum.

Acquisition model

The likely route is system/pitch-led discovery: an immediately legible “Galcon × tower defense” formulation, a compact GIF/screenshot-friendly map, and direct browser access. There is no evidence that known IP, AI novelty or creator celebrity is the acquisition engine.

Recommended growth assets

  1. A 12–18 second silent clip: neutral capture → supply activation → horde → last-second hold.
  2. A daily seed card with map silhouette, ruleset, completion state and shareable URL.
  3. A public “Can you hold 12 waves?” browser challenge that starts directly in the map.
  4. Post-launch changelogs that quote player feedback themes and show the resulting UI fix.

14 · Development model

What the product reveals—and what it does not

Supported inferences

  • System-first design: multiple modes reuse one node/route simulation.
  • Data-oriented content: campaign missions differ by topology, wave quota, rival count and rules rather than bespoke rendering.
  • Deterministic or reproducible setup is likely because results expose map seeds.
  • The browser build uses responsive full-viewport composition and a reusable modal/panel system.
  • Accessibility-conscious tempo controls are built into the primary HUD.

Not disclosed

  • Engine, framework and backend architecture.
  • Team size, development duration and production budget.
  • Procedural generation algorithm and AI design.
  • Testing process, analytics and iteration history.
  • Use of generative AI in code, art, audio or design.
Important boundary. “Generated soundtrack” is credited to Suno in the field guide. That supports one narrow audio-production fact; it does not establish an AI-first production process for the rest of the game.

17 · Acceptance criteria

Definition of reconstruction-complete

Functional

  • All nodes are connected by a validated graph and accept only legal sends.
  • Drag-send exposes the committed and remaining troop counts before release.
  • Troops travel, collide with defense, capture nodes and recolor ownership.
  • Wave director transitions through warning, active and resolved states.
  • Ringhold HP, repairs and loss condition operate separately from garrison.
  • Supply route activates, displays direction and stops under documented conditions.
  • Pause and all speed steps work without desynchronizing simulation.
  • Campaign results unlock the next mission and persist earned stars.
  • Freeplay can reproduce a map from its displayed seed.

UX / visual

  • Player, enemy and neutral states remain distinguishable in grayscale through icon/shape.
  • Top HUD fits at 1280×720 without collisions; critical controls remain visible.
  • Every interactive node has at least a 44×44 px hit region.
  • No transient banner covers its referenced target at common aspect ratios.
  • Result modals are keyboard navigable and focus is trapped while open.
  • Guide panel works at 200% zoom without clipped controls.
  • Animation honors reduced-motion preference.
  • Audio channels preserve master × category volume behavior.

Test scenarios

  1. Send a partial army across one edge, cancel a second send, then commit all with the modifier.
  2. Capture a neutral branching node, create supply, fill the target and verify flow stops.
  3. Survive the final campaign wave with low base HP, record stars, continue, then lose; the clear must remain persistent.
  4. Enable co-pilot in freeplay, verify persistent disclosure, finish the run and mark it unscored.
  5. Run at 0.5×, pause mid-march, resume at 4× and verify deterministic arrival order.
