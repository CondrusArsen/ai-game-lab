01
Executive verdict

A tiny rule with excellent compression.

Three ordered letters × open-vocabulary search × sixty-second score chase.

Observed Expandagram explains itself in one screen, accepts a word in one field, gives immediate green/red feedback and closes with a meaningful result card. The full loop is understandable without a tutorial sequence.

Hypothesis Its long-term ceiling depends less on feature volume than on dictionary trust, challenge curation and a social reason to compare answers. A disputed rejection can outweigh several correct accepts.

Study it — the constraint is the product

Best asset

A clean mental model: the three letters must appear in order, but other letters may sit anywhere around or between them.

Main risk

The dictionary and validation explanation are invisible infrastructure. If either feels arbitrary, mastery becomes argument.

Product posture

Strong microgame and daily-challenge candidate; no evidence yet for durable retention or social scale.

03
What the game is

A constrained vocabulary sprint, not an anagram.

Genre

Single-player timed word-finding score chaser with an ordered-subsequence constraint.

Perspective

Static, centered, form-like interface. The play space is linguistic rather than spatial.

Goal

Submit as many valid, non-repeated words as possible before the countdown ends.

Core constraint

A word must contain all three GRAM letters in the displayed order. Other letters may precede, separate or follow them.

Scoring fantasy

Longer discoveries outperform short safe words; the result reveals one stronger answer the player missed.

Audience

Hypothesis Fans of word ladders, daily word puzzles and fast solo score comparison rather than crossword-style clue solving.

04
Observed first session

Understand, hunt, validate, review, restart.

1 · Learn

The start screen defines a GRAM, shows ordered-letter examples and states the time, length bonus and no-repeat rule.

2 · Start

A new three-letter sequence appears with a full countdown, focused input, Shuffle, New Game and Submit.

3 · First answers

Valid words immediately increase score and word count, show a green equation and enter the ledger.

4 · Invalid order

An English-looking answer with the letters in the wrong order receives a red message naming the violated GRAM.

5 · Continue

The player accumulates five accepted words while the timer colour shifts from black to amber to red.

6 · Result

At expiry, entry controls disappear. Score, word count, scored answers, a stronger missed word and personal records appear.

7 · Play again

A fresh GRAM starts with an empty ledger, zero score and a reset timer.

8 · Shuffle

Shuffle reorders the same three letters during an active round; timer and current progress remain in place.

05
Core loop

Scan the lexicon in your head before the clock closes.

Read GRAM

Encode the three displayed letters and their left-to-right order.

Generate

Search memory for a word containing that ordered subsequence.

Type

Enter one candidate in the single text field.

Validate

Submit; receive acceptance with points or a specific rejection.

Adapt

Use the growing ledger, remaining time and optional Shuffle to change search strategy.

Review

At expiry, inspect total, best discoveries and one high-value missed answer; play again.

Observed One complete timed round and the start of a replay establish the full cycle. Unknown Candidate generation, dictionary source and GRAM difficulty balancing are not visible systems.

08
Progression, economy and content

Progress is score memory, not an economy.

Within-round

Score, word count, accepted-word ledger and shrinking time create immediate progression. Longer words add bonus value.

Result mastery

The final list marks bonus-scoring words and suggests a higher-value answer the player could also have found.

Personal records

“All-time best score” and “all-time best word” persist across the observed transition. Storage scope is not established.

Content unit

Each GRAM is a lightweight challenge seed whose quality depends on answer breadth and difficulty.

Unlocks

Unknown No levels, streaks, achievements, cosmetics, currency or gated modes were visible.

Monetization

Unknown No ads, payments, premium tier or purchase flow appeared.

Expansion cost: medium. Adding GRAMs is computationally cheap but editorially expensive: every seed needs answer coverage, difficulty scoring, offensive-word policy and dictionary-version testing.

Three screen families and four decisive response states.

The interface is desktop-sparse but structurally narrow enough for mobile adaptation. All dimensions below are relational, not claims about original pixels.

A · Instruction / start

Purpose
Explain the complete rule and establish the visual language before timing begins.
Composition
Narrow centered column in the upper half; spaced wordmark, grey instruction card, sample GRAM, shuffle preview, three accepted examples, rules card and full-width black CTA.
Visible copy
“This is a GRAM.” “Expand a GRAM to make words.” “Letters must stay in order.” “One minute per game.” “Bonus points for longer words.” “Each word can be used once.”
States
Default, hover, keyboard focus and loading-start are required; only default is visible.

B · Active round / empty

Purpose
Start the timed search with zero history and immediate input focus.
Composition
Wordmark, large timer, GRAM, full-width Shuffle, bordered input, split New Game / Submit row, score and word counters, then ledger.
Visible copy
“Shuffle.” “type a word.” “New Game.” “Submit.” “Score: 0.” “Words: 0.” “no words yet.”
States
Fresh, typing, submitting, accepted, rejected, duplicate, dictionary failure, expiring and expired.

C · Active round / response

Purpose
Teach validity while maintaining typing speed.

Green uppercase word, checkmark and score equation; new ledger row; counters increase.

Red uppercase word plus specific reason; score and ledger unchanged.
Behavior
Input clears, focus should return, message occupies a stable region to avoid layout shift.
Data
candidate, normalized word, rule result, dictionary result, duplicate result, base, bonus and total.

D · Result / records

Purpose
Summarize performance, teach one better answer and offer immediate replay.
Composition
Play Again above GRAM; grey summary card with large score and answer table; “you could also have found” prompt; mint record cards below.
Visible copy

Interaction
Play Again starts fresh; suggested answer cycles on tap according to visible hint.
States
new record, existing record, no accepted words and no suggestion available are necessary but not all observed.

10
Feedback and presentation

Text does nearly all the work—and mostly succeeds.

Success language

Green text combines accepted word, checkmark and arithmetic. The ledger makes the outcome persistent instead of transient.

Error language

Red copy names the submitted word and the ordered-letter rule. It is specific enough to teach rather than merely reject.

Urgency language

The countdown changes from black to amber and then red. No additional motion or meter competes with word recall.

Result language

Large score and word count give closure; a missed high-value word reframes failure as discovery.

Motion

Unknown Screenshots establish state changes, not animation timing. Keep transitions near-instant to protect typing rhythm.

Sound

Unknown No reliable audio contract is available. If added, success, rejection, warning and expiry must remain optional and non-blocking.

11
Social, viral and return loops

Replay exists; ritual and comparison do not yet surface.

Immediate replay

Play Again is the first result-page action. A new GRAM and clean slate appear immediately.

Mastery

Players improve at subsequence search, rare-word recall, length-risk selection and strategic shuffling.

Personal status

All-time best score and best word provide lightweight persistence without an account.

Teaching return

The missed-word card exposes a superior answer, creating a small “I could have seen that” loop.

Social surface

No leaderboard, challenge link, share card, multiplayer or friend comparison is visible.

Daily ritual

No date-keyed GRAM, streak, archive or common seed is shown. Adding one is a product experiment, not reconstruction.

12
Strengths and frictions

Minimalism sharpens both the rule and every omission.

What works

  • One-screen onboarding: examples demonstrate the non-obvious order rule.
  • Zero navigation tax: the primary field is focused at round start.
  • Specific feedback: invalid-order copy exposes the exact failure.
  • Transparent score: letters, bonus and total remain inspectable.
  • Useful result: a missed answer teaches the solution space.
  • Replay speed: one prominent action returns to a fresh constraint.

What needs attention

  • P0 Dictionary source and word-acceptance policy are invisible.
  • P0 New Game can discard a live attempt with no observed confirmation.
  • P1 Shuffle’s strategic cost, limit and effect on accepted words are unexplained.
  • P1 No duplicate-word rejection was demonstrated despite the rule.
  • P1 No pause/focus-loss policy protects a timed run.
  • P2 The result offers only one missed answer at a time and no shareable summary.

Make validation deterministic and explainable.

Mandatory behavior

Validation truth

  • Normalize case and whitespace consistently.
  • Run subsequence order independently from dictionary membership.
  • Reject duplicates without changing score or order.
  • Pin dictionary and score versions for replayable tests.

Clock integrity

  • Use a monotonic deadline, not decrement-only intervals.
  • Disable submission atomically at expiry.
  • Compute results from the accepted ledger snapshot.
  • Define focus-loss/background-tab policy explicitly.

Feedback contract

  • Keep response region stable to prevent layout shift.
  • Announce success and failure to assistive technology.
  • Show base, bonus and total on every acceptance.
  • Return focus to input while the round remains active.

Unknown boundary

  • Do not invent the original dictionary provider.
  • Do not extrapolate the entire bonus table from two lengths.
  • Do not claim cloud persistence for all-time records.
  • Do not add daily/social systems to parity scope.

Vertical slices

1 · Rule engine

GRAM subsequence validator, normalization, duplicates and versioned score table with deterministic fixtures.

2 · Timed shell

Instructions, active form, countdown urgency, accepted ledger, failure reasons and expiry.

3 · Result memory

Round summary, missed suggestion, local best score/best word and Play Again.

4 · Content QA

GRAM generator, answer coverage, difficulty estimates, exclusions and dictionary regression suite.

Acceptance specification

Test the rule before testing the polish.

First entry. Given a fresh visitor, when the page loads, then the wordmark, how-to card, sample GRAM, three valid examples, three rules and Start Game are visible in one clear column.
Start game. Given the instruction screen, when Start Game is activated, then a new GRAM appears, the countdown begins, score and words are zero, the ledger says “no words yet.” and focus enters the input.
Ordered subsequence. Given GRAM F·L·N, when FLAN is submitted, then it is accepted because F, L and N occur in order despite other letters.
Wrong order. Given GRAM F·L·N, when FINALLY is submitted, then score remains unchanged and the red message says it does not contain F·L·N in order.
Dictionary failure. Given a correctly ordered non-word, when submitted, then a dictionary-specific rejection appears without claiming the order is wrong.
Duplicate. Given an already accepted normalized word, when it is submitted again with different case or surrounding spaces, then the duplicate rule rejects it and score does not change.
Base scoring. Given an accepted four-letter word, when validation succeeds, then base points equal four, bonus is zero and total is four.
Bonus scoring. Given an accepted six-letter word under the observed rules version, when validation succeeds, then the ledger shows six plus one equals seven.
Success feedback. Given a valid unique word, when submitted, then green feedback names the word and arithmetic, counters update once, a ledger row appears and input focus returns.
Shuffle. Given active GRAM BST, when Shuffle is activated, then the same letters appear in a different order, the countdown continues and existing score/ledger remain intact.
New Game. Given a live attempt with progress, when New Game is confirmed, then a new GRAM starts with zero score, zero words, empty ledger and a full countdown.
Expiry race. Given a candidate submission crossing the deadline, when validation and expiry compete, then exactly one server/client-authoritative outcome is committed and no post-expiry score appears.
Result. Given the deadline expires, when the round closes, then entry controls are replaced by Play Again, total score, word count, immutable answer ledger, one eligible missed suggestion and personal-best cards.
Best-word tie. Given a word tying the stored best points, when results persist, then the documented stable tie policy determines whether the displayed best changes.
Play Again. Given the result screen, when Play Again is activated, then a fresh round starts once and no stale status message or answer survives.
Accessibility. All primary actions are keyboard reachable; focused controls are visible; status is announced; timer colour is not the sole expiry cue.
Visual regression. At wide and narrow widths, compare instruction, empty, typed, success, error, critical timer, result, new game and shuffled states for column alignment, stable status height and zero viewport overflow.
18
Experiments

Build comparison and trust around the existing minute.

1 · Shareable result

Hypothesis: a compact text/image summary will convert private vocabulary wins into organic challenges.

Test: share GRAM, score, word count and length distribution while hiding the full answer list by default.

Success: share rate and referred completed rounds.

False read: novelty sharing may spike without repeat play.

2 · Daily common GRAM

Hypothesis: one shared seed creates fair comparison and a return ritual.

Test: add a daily mode beside unlimited random play.

Success: next-day return and completion among daily starters.

False read: reminders can lift returns independently of puzzle quality.

3 · Dictionary transparency

Hypothesis: reason codes plus “report word” reduce frustration from disputed vocabulary.

Test: show accepted locale/rules and allow one-tap appeal after unknown-word rejection.

Success: fewer abandonment events after rejection and lower repeated-submit rate.

False read: appeals may increase because the affordance advertises disagreement.

4 · Shuffle framing

Hypothesis: explicitly calling Shuffle a free reordering tool improves strategic use without confusing the rule.

Test: compare current label with “Reorder these letters” and a first-use tooltip.

Success: more useful shuffle use and fewer post-shuffle invalid-order submissions.

False read: extra copy may slow experienced players.

5 · Result exploration

Hypothesis: a small carousel of missed words creates learning value and replay intent.

Test: expose three suggestions ranked by length instead of one, with definitions behind tap.

Success: suggestion interaction and Play Again conversion.

False read: curiosity about definitions may not translate to play.

Confidence and unknowns

High confidence in the minute; low confidence in the lexicon beneath it.

High confidence

  • Instruction copy and ordered-letter rule.
  • One-minute round structure.
  • Start, input, Submit, Shuffle, New Game and Play Again states.
  • Valid and invalid-order feedback.
  • Observed length scoring examples.
  • Result summary, missed suggestion and personal record cards.

Medium confidence

  • Records persist locally across rounds.
  • Shuffle is unlimited and free.
  • Score base always equals word length.
  • Suggestions are drawn from the same dictionary.
  • Difficulty is determined by answer-set size and familiarity.

Unknown

  • Dictionary source, locale and inflection rules.
  • Complete length-bonus schedule.
  • Duplicate and non-word error copy.
  • New Game confirmation and focus-loss behavior.
  • Record storage lifetime and privacy.
  • GRAM generation, exclusions and difficulty balance.
  • Audio, analytics, backend, hosting and complete stack.

Evidence boundary

Observed The complete first round, response states, result, restart and shuffle come from direct product interaction. Visible Exact rule, component hierarchy, examples, score equations and result copy come from the UI states. Radar Creator, launch metrics, origin story and four-day build claim come from the dated creator-source record. Hypothesis Audience fit, growth drivers, implementation choices and experiments are product interpretations. Unknown Hidden dictionary, storage and architecture are not asserted.

Bottom line. Expandagram is reconstruction-ready as an offline single-player minute with observed states and scoring examples. Exact parity still requires the original dictionary policy, the full bonus table, duplicate/non-word responses, New Game confirmation, focus-loss behavior and persistence scope.
