01
Executive verdict

The habit is the upgrade currency—and it cannot be ground.

1/day

Progress rate

Visible One kept promise produces one layer. The next opportunity arrives at the daily UTC boundary.

90

Declared horizon

Nine shield materials and ability gates map a long real-world commitment onto a finite level ladder.

2

Coupled loops

The daily ritual creates strength; a replayable arena makes that strength legible immediately through survival, score and waves.

Verdict — worth studying. 90to90 has a rare, coherent product metaphor: personal consistency literally forges defense. Its strongest idea is the non-grindable link between a private promise and a public, game-readable object. Its greatest risk is delayed proof—new players must wait days before the shield fantasy, arena depth and unlocked abilities become meaningfully different.

Protect

  • The promise remains private while the shield can be compared.
  • The daily layer, material ladder and arena stats share one progress number.
  • Failure is repairable: grace marks and healing scars preserve continuity.
  • The arena is playable repeatedly without manufacturing fake habit progress.

Resolve

  • Explain exactly how early levels change survivability.
  • Separate per-run values from all-time bests on the result screen.
  • Make emotional safety explicit around “broken promise” language.
  • Prove a satisfying day-two and week-one return, not just an elegant day-one concept.

03
What the game is

A private habit tracker with a public-facing shield and a survivor-like proof arena.

Genre

Habit commitment product fused with shield crafting, 90-step meta progression and top-down wave survival.

Fantasy

Every honest day adds material to a shield. What the player practices outside the game becomes what protects them inside it.

Audience

Self-improvement users who respond to game identity and visible mastery more than streak charts; keyboard-friendly arcade players are the secondary audience.

Business model

Visible No ads, store or purchase surface appeared. The creator says all 90 levels are free; monetization remains unknown.

Privacy is part of the product promise. The habit text is framed as private; leaderboard copy explicitly says the promise stays private. Save/restore later introduces server-linked progress, so privacy and data-lifecycle language must remain consistent across onboarding, settings and deletion.
04
Observed first session

The first visit completes both loops, records a failure honestly and joins the social layer.

Understand

Landing promises a paper-to-diamond transformation over ninety days and a free, accountless start.

Commit

Enter one short private promise and create the first shield layer.

Learn

Six modal steps explain daily layers, UTC reset, three forgiven days, arena strength and scars.

Forge

Strike the paper shield once; level becomes 1/90, progress 1.11%, and no upgrades remain today.

Prove

Enter the arena, move while attacks auto-target, survive waves, lose, retry and set a stronger best.

Extend

Inspect cross-device recovery and accessibility, record a healing scar, then join the pseudonymous leaderboard.

Not reached The visit did not return on a later day, consume a grace mark through absence, lose levels after all grace is gone, heal a scar, cross a material threshold, unlock an active ability, install the web app, restore another device or end/delete the challenge.

Decision

The meta decision is honesty—kept, missed or explicitly broken. The arena decision is movement: route around enemies and projectiles while automatic targeting handles offense.

Payoff

The shield changes visibly, the level bar advances, arena stats improve by design intent, abilities unlock on a schedule, and performance is summarized by score, wave and survival.

Reason to return

Progress cannot be accelerated through play. A countdown, optional private reminder, material milestones and the next ability create tomorrow-facing tension.

08
Progression, economy and content

The economy is time and honesty; content unlocks every ten levels.

Material ladder

Visible Paper 10, Cardboard 20, Wood 30, Gold 40, Bronze 50, Iron 60, Steel 70, Titanium 80 and Diamond 90.

Ability ladder

Truth strike is available immediately. Dash unlocks at 11; Parry 21; Deflect 31; Shockwave 41; Time slows 51; Magnet 61; Fast recovery 71; Nova 81.

Early bonus gate

Focus Attack is advertised to unlock at level 3. A later arena status shows “Truth edge · 6.0s,” but its relationship to Focus is not established.

Progress sink

There is no spendable soft currency. Missed days can consume grace and later reduce level; explicit breaks add recoverable scars.

Content cadence

Ability gates imply a new action roughly every ten levels. Materials visualize decade-scale status even when daily interactions remain the same.

Expansion cost

Hypothesis Medium: new waves/enemies are cheaper than new habit mechanics, but 90 days require balanced stat curves, nine shield art sets and long-horizon integrity testing.

Visual inconsistency to resolve. The landing illustration labels a gold shield at Day 31, while the dashboard’s milestone strip associates Gold with 40. The landing may be illustrative, but a reconstruction should align all milestone labels or explicitly mark promotional art as a sample.

No purchase currency, paywall, ad, energy or boost store appeared. According to the creator’s public post, the playable 90-level version is free. That is a product claim—not proof that future monetization will remain absent.

10
Feedback and presentation

Quiet ritual outside; escalating pressure inside.

Shield strike

A centered hit point and sound make the daily action physical. Layer-added copy, level change and spent-upgrade state close the transaction.

Material identity

Pixel shields share a silhouette while texture, hue and hardware evolve. The object acts as progress bar, avatar and collectible.

Arena pressure

Wave cards name escalation; new enemy colors and projectiles expand the field; combo and score reward continuous survival.

Damage and loss

Health pips diminish and the avatar visibly reacts. The blurred arena behind “The shield fell” preserves the cause while foregrounding the outcome.

Audio has both a global top-bar toggle and a Settings preference. Reduced motion is explicitly supported and follows the system setting by default—an unusually strong accessibility signal for a small browser game. Exact music, sound mix and all animation timings remain unverified.

11
Social, viral and return loops

The product is private by default and comparable by choice.

Daily return

Countdown to the next UTC layer, a 90-step journey and optional reminder create a calendar-based return. The reminder arrives about eight hours before deadline and does not reveal the promise.

Mastery return

Arena best score, furthest wave, combo control and future abilities let the player test improvement without accelerating the habit layer.

Optional social proof

Leaderboard participation requires only a 2–20 character alias. It exposes shield material, level and furthest wave—not the promise.

Portability

A private recovery code links browsers to the same server journey. No account is required, but continuity still has an explicit transfer mechanism.

Retention unknown The observed affordances are credible reasons to return, but no later-day behavior, notification delivery, cohort retention or long-term leaderboard population was measured.

17
Acceptance specification

Behavioral parity is proven through journeys, not isolated screens.

First entry. Given no journey, when landing loads, then the paper-to-diamond promise, free/no-account cue and Build your shield CTA are visible.
Promise validation. Given an empty or whitespace-only promise, when submitted, then creation is blocked with accessible inline feedback and nothing public is created.
First layer. Given one available upgrade, when the shield is struck once, then level becomes 1/90, progress 1.11%, availability becomes zero and countdown targets the next UTC boundary.
Idempotency. Given today’s layer is spent, when repeated clicks or reloads occur, then no second layer is granted.
Grace consequence. Given a missed day and grace remaining, when the ledger resolves it, then exactly one mark is consumed and level is not reduced.
Depleted grace. Given zero grace marks, when one missed day resolves, then exactly three levels are removed without crossing below zero.
Scar recovery. Given an explicit broken-promise confirmation, then one independent scar appears; after its recovery deadline it disappears without resetting other timers.
Arena preparation. Given level 1, then Paper, 1/90, Health 2, Shield 0, Strike ×0.67, Fire rate 1.01s and the visible ability gates match the observed screen.
Core combat. Given a started run, WASD and arrows move at equal normalized speed while Truth strike targets the nearest eligible enemy automatically.
Damage / invalid state. Given projectile contact, health decreases once with non-color feedback; at zero, simulation stops and the result screen appears once.
Pause / resume. Given an active run, when Pause is chosen, then simulation, buffs and wave timers freeze; Resume continues from the same state.
Retry. Given a completed run, when Again is chosen, run-only score/health/wave reset while best score and furthest wave persist.
Result clarity. Given a worse later run, then current score/survival and all-time best score/wave are labelled separately.
Leaderboard privacy. Given a joined alias, public rows contain alias, shield, level and furthest wave; the private promise never appears in markup, requests or logs.
Recovery. Given a valid exported code on another browser, Import links the same journey; invalid, revoked and offline states are explicit and non-destructive.
Preferences. Sound and reduced motion apply immediately; reduced motion suppresses nonessential shake/glow while preserving state feedback.
Reminder. Given permission, the reminder arrives near the declared pre-deadline window and never includes promise text.
Destructive actions. End challenge and Delete data require separate confirmations; deletion removes server journey and invalidates recovery codes.
Visual regression. Landing, promise form, tutorial modal, available/spent/scarred shield, arena prep/run/result, leaderboard and both Settings regions match at wide and narrow widths.
18
Product experiments

Test week-one motivation without compromising the daily contract.

Experiment 01

Day-two proof preview

After the first layer, show a non-interactive before/after of tomorrow’s shield and exact stat delta.

Hypothesis: concrete anticipation raises next-day return.

Test: preview vs current countdown-only state. Success: valid next-period check-in rate. False-read risk: notification opt-in or timezone mix can masquerade as motivation.

Experiment 02

Practice power sampler

Offer one clearly labelled demo run at level 11 without changing journey progress or leaderboard eligibility.

Hypothesis: tasting Dash makes the ten-day horizon meaningful.

Test: optional demo after first death. Success: week-one return and completed daily claims. False-read risk: demo novelty may temporarily inflate sessions without improving habit adherence.

Experiment 03

Run / best result split

Show “This run” and “Personal best” as two explicit rows.

Hypothesis: clear metric scope increases intentional retries.

Test: relabel result only. Success: retry rate after non-record run and comprehension check. False-read risk: layout prominence, not comprehension, may drive clicks.

Experiment 04

Compassionate repair language

Test “Record a setback” against “I broke my promise,” keeping consequence and healing identical.

Hypothesis: neutral language reduces abandonment while preserving honesty.

Test: copy-only randomized trial. Success: continuation after recording, later honest check-ins, low correction rate. False-read risk: lower scar use could mean avoidance, not healthier framing.

Experiment 05

Reminder timing choice

Let players choose a local reminder window while retaining the UTC deadline explanation.

Hypothesis: user-timed prompts improve valid check-ins without exposing the promise.

Test: fixed eight-hour lead vs selected window. Success: on-time completion and notification retention. False-read risk: opt-in selection bias.
